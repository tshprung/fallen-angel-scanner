# Historical validation plan

This is the validation protocol for the [offline runner](BACKTEST.md). No historical dataset, measured edge or survival estimate is included. The forward ledger is a smoke test of the observation workflow; it is not a portfolio backtest.

1. **Freeze the research question and protocol.** Start with US ordinary equities, where point-in-time coverage and delisting data can be audited. Specify a universe, signal cutoff, sizing, cash rule, issuer exposure limits, executable entry/exit rules, cost assumptions and missing-data treatment before seeing test-period results. Keep six-, twelve- and twenty-four-month cohorts separate. Report +50% within twelve months and +100% within twenty-four months, distinguishing threshold touches, executable exits and terminal returns. Treat bankruptcy, distressed delisting, acquisition and ordinary delisting as distinct outcomes. The five-year distress analysis needs separately matured cohorts.
2. **Build auditable, point-in-time inputs.** Use dated universe membership and stable security/issuer IDs, including delisted names and ticker reuse. Store statement versions, first public availability timestamps and source/accession IDs; never backdate a restatement to its fiscal period. Store exchange sessions, raw prices, splits, dividends, rights, spin-offs, FX, and merger/delisting consideration. Build deterministic replay fixtures for a late filing, renamed security, missing next open, special dividend, reverse split, cash and stock mergers, and a distressed delisting. Reconcile adjusted returns against a cash-and-shares ledger on those fixtures. Log missing outcomes and report sensitivity bounds rather than deleting them.
3. **Simulate executable portfolios and matched alternatives.** Replay the frozen scanner using only facts known by each signal cutoff. Enter at the next executable regular session under a declared missing-open rule. Apply spread, commission, slippage, market impact and capacity limits with cost stress tests. Compare against a broad total-return benchmark, drawdown-only, value/quality without drawdown, and size/sector/liquidity-matched contemporaneous candidates. Give strategies the same universe, timing, constraints and trading budget. Evaluate filter ablations; don't attribute market or sector exposure to the scanner.
4. **Use time-ordered evaluation and a decision gate.** Separate development, validation and a locked final holdout; purge overlapping label windows at fold boundaries. Report net excess return, drawdown, turnover, severe-loss rate, target hit/exit rates, outcome coverage and uncertainty using date blocks and repeated-issuer sensitivity checks. Declare economic and statistical acceptance criteria before looking at the holdout; track all variants tested. Require results robust to realistic costs, regime splits and unresolved terminal outcomes. If sample size or coverage cannot support a conclusion, keep the system in research mode.
5. **Test AI separately in prospective shadow mode.** Historical web queries or current models may know later outcomes, so exclude them from the initial historical test. Freeze prompts and source cutoffs, timestamp decisions, and compare AI-assisted and deterministic queues on the same candidates and review budget. Measure claim accuracy, time saved, incremental net returns and severe losses once outcomes mature.
